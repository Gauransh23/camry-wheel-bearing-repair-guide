FIRST LOOK

See how the parts fit together

Start with the hub parts, then add the knuckle, steering, suspension and brakes. Dashed lines identify a shared axis; exploded spacing does not specify installation order.

Bearing and knuckle
The bearing's outer steel ring fits the knuckle bore. Its inner rings receive the hub stem. The retaining snap ring belongs to the knuckle groove; the small dust deflector is a separate part.
Hub, wheel studs and rotor
The hub stem enters the bearing from outboard. The rotor's five holes slide over the five hub studs. The wheel nuts later clamp the wheel and rotor at the hub; they differ from the central axle nut.
CV axle and central nut B
The splined outer CV stub passes through the hub's splined center. Nut B threads onto that axle stub and establishes the required clamp; it does not thread onto the hub itself.
Connections around the knuckle
H: two strut through-bolts. F: tie-rod taper at the steering arm. G: ball-joint flange to lower arm, one bolt plus two nuts. C: two carrier-to-knuckle bolts. D: separate ABS sensor. E: hose/wire bracket on the strut. Locate each in the connection map and fastener reference.

DIY SETUP

Use what you already have

No lift, air tools or hydraulic shop press is assumed in this chapter.

Owned hand tools

Ratchet, extensions, breaker bar; sockets/wrenches 10, 12, 17, 19, 21, 22 mm; 30 mm deep 12-point axle socket. Needle-nose pliers, side cutters, hammer, suitable punch/staking tool, soft mallet, trim pick, marker, brush, cloths and non-residue cleaner. A small amount of engine oil is needed only for the cited reused strut-nut-thread condition.

19 mm is useful to have for changed hardware; the main-route original-style joints are identified individually in the table.

Support & joint tools

Floor jack, rated stands, proper saddles, wheel chocks, caliper hanger, secure axle/knuckle support and a boot-safe screw-type tie-rod separator. A simple separator may be owned cheaply or borrowed; check it fits before dismantling.

A suitable pedal depressor keeps the axle-nut steps a solo task. A helper can also hold the pedal for those two operations.

Your loaner torque wrench

The 250 ft·lb maximum covers 217 ft·lb. Confirm its lower range covers the other settings. Match socket drive sizes without improvised extensions and follow its grip/calibration instructions.

Also needed: usable coverage for 71 in·lb / 8 N·m and 14 ft·lb / 19 N·m. Check the 36 ft·lb tie-rod setting too; borrow a middle-range instrument if the small/large ranges do not overlap.

Measurement & bench tools

A dial indicator and rigid mount are required to complete at-home hub acceptance; otherwise arrange this inspection before dismantling. A micrometer checks rotor thickness. Alignment and ABS checks may require equipment or a service beyond a normal socket kit.

The identified loaner is OEMTOOLS 57342 / AutoZone SKU 347191. Add snap-ring pliers, a caliper, retained bench support/soft jaws and compatible screw lubricant. Reusing a hub with a stuck inner race may require a separate splitter/puller. The 27213 reference manual is not proof of identical 57342 adapters.

New pieces / consumables

Correct new axle nut; new tie-rod cotter pin; VIN-matched bearing, serviceable hub, NEW snap ring and NEW No.1 bearing dust deflector, plus a NEW ball-joint cotter pin for the bench operation. Have replacement clips or damaged bolts available if inspection calls for them. Keep the sealed bearing clean and packaged until its outer-ring-only installation stack is ready.

WHEN IT BINDS

Change the method before adding force

Common driveway obstacles and the decision that keeps the next step controlled.

ProblemFirst useful checkDecision / limit
Axle nut will not turnIs the stake completely lifted? Is the 30 mm socket fully engaged? Does the brake hold?Resolve locking/thread damage. Do not pull so hard that stands shift.
ABS sensor stuck in knuckleCheck corrosion around the boss and whether the body moves without cable strain.Plan safe extraction or replacement. Unplugging alone leaves it vulnerable.
Rotor rusted to hubClean the center pilot; inspect intended release holes and verify their threads.Use matched release bolts alternately. Do not pound studs or bend shield.
Tie-rod taper stuckCheck separator fit, central screw contact, boot clearance and retention.Use controlled separator load. A torn boot changes the repair scope.
Axle splines seizedCheck stake/thread issues and spline corrosion; ensure shaft support.Use a suitable hub-mounted axle pusher. No hard steel blows or uncontrolled CV pulling.
Strut bolts trappedReposition supported knuckle to unload side force on each bolt.Do not enlarge holes, bend strut ears or hammer threaded ends bare.
Hub has play/runout after refitCheck fixture, clean face, fully seated parts, axle clamp and completed bench work.Investigate bearing/hub installation or damaged parts. Do not overtorque the nut.
ABS/VSC light or brake faultInspect seating, clips, connector, rotor condition and read ABS-specific faults.Resolve the fault and applicable signal/calibration checks before declaring completion.
